2026 guide

The best OpenRouter alternative depends on why you are changing. Choose TrustedRouter for a hosted multi-provider API with privacy you can verify. Choose LiteLLM when your team wants to operate its own gateway. Choose Portkey for mature gateway controls and observability. Choose Vercel AI Gateway for a Vercel and AI SDK centered stack. Choose Cloudflare AI Gateway for edge controls. Choose Bedrock or Vertex AI when cloud procurement and IAM matter more than portability.

OpenRouter remains a strong choice for catalog breadth and a large developer ecosystem. A useful comparison should make that clear. The alternatives below solve different problems, so the right decision starts with deployment model, privacy boundary, provider access, fallback, and operational ownership.

Hosted and private

TrustedRouter

TrustedRouter is the closest fit when you want OpenRouter-style hosted model access with a different trust boundary. The gateway is source-visible and hardware-attested. It stores metadata for billing and reliability while prompt and output content remain ephemeral. Downstream retention and confidential-compute claims stay route-specific and visible on model and provider pages.

Self-hosted

LiteLLM

LiteLLM is the practical answer when your team wants an OpenAI-compatible proxy under its own operational control. It translates provider APIs and includes budgets, rate limits, and cost tracking. That control comes with responsibility for deployment, scaling, secrets, provider accounts, and incident response.

TrustedRouter vs LiteLLM

Controls

Portkey and Helicone

Portkey is strongest when policy, fallbacks, budgets, and gateway observability are the center of the decision. Helicone is strongest when tracing, cost analysis, and request debugging are the center. Both can fit teams that already have provider relationships. Review logging and content capture settings before sending sensitive traffic.

Framework native

Vercel AI Gateway

Vercel AI Gateway offers one endpoint, model routing, budgets, monitoring, and provider fallback. It is especially convenient for applications already built with the AI SDK or hosted on Vercel. Teams outside that stack should compare the convenience against a provider-neutral gateway.

TrustedRouter vs Vercel AI Gateway

Edge

Cloudflare AI Gateway

Cloudflare AI Gateway puts analytics, caching, rate limits, retries, and fallback close to Cloudflare's network. It is compelling when those edge controls are already part of your architecture. Its product shape is a gateway layer first, rather than a broad model marketplace first.

TrustedRouter vs Cloudflare AI Gateway

Cloud native

Bedrock and Vertex AI

Amazon Bedrock and Google Vertex AI fit organizations that want model access inside an existing cloud account, IAM structure, procurement process, and regional footprint. They trade some portability for cloud-native governance. Model names, regions, quotas, and compatibility vary by platform.

Decision guide

How to choose an OpenRouter alternative.

Start with the deployment model. Decide whether you want a hosted marketplace, a self-hosted proxy, a cloud-native service, or direct provider integrations.

Define the privacy boundary. Separate gateway content handling from the downstream provider's retention and confidential-compute posture. Ask what can be verified technically and what remains contractual.

Test failure behavior. Confirm which errors trigger retries, whether fallback crosses providers, and how duplicate billing is prevented.

Run a small task-specific eval. Price and benchmark tables narrow the field. Your own prompts reveal the quality, latency, and reliability that matter in production.

Check operating cost. Include platform fees, provider markup, engineering ownership, observability, support, and the cost of maintaining several integrations.
