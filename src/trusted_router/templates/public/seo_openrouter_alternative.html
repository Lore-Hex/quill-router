Zero retention, attested, encrypted: what each label means.

Zero data retention (ZDR).

The model provider's terms say it won't store your prompts or outputs. It's a contractual promise. TrustedRouter labels which routes have one, and "provider": {"min_privacy": "zdr"} refuses any route that doesn't.

Attested gateway.

TrustedRouter's own gateway runs on confidential hardware that proves which published build is handling your request, and that build keeps no prompt or output logs. Attestation covers our hop, so the model provider's terms still apply.

Confidential, end-to-end encrypted routes.

The prompt stays encrypted until it reaches a provider's verified confidential-computing hardware, and the route must also be zero-retention. Select them with "min_privacy": "confidential" or the trustedrouter/e2e model. Available on open-weight models today.

Five questions to ask any OpenRouter alternative.

Can you prove which code handles my prompts?

A policy says what a vendor intends. TrustedRouter publishes a hardware attestation of the exact build handling your requests, and that build doesn't log prompts.
